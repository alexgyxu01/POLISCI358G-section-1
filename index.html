About

I am a master’s student in East Asian Studies, with a background in statistics and political science. My work sits between the two: I read Chinese policy and legal material closely, and I use quantitative methods to test claims about it that are usually argued qualitatively.

Research interests

My substantive focus is data governance and AI policy in China — how rules over data collection, storage, and cross-border transfer are written, revised, and enforced, and what that implies for the firms and agencies operating under them.

Methodologically I work in statistics, primarily causal inference and econometric approaches: identifying plausible sources of variation in when and where policy takes effect, and estimating what actually changes as a result.

Project ideas

My current project asks how AI is reshaping data governance in China. Questions I want to work through:

  • Whether the push to build AI capacity loosens data rules in practice, even where the formal regime tightens on paper.
  • How training-data demand changes what public and private actors are willing to share, and on what terms.
  • Whether enforcement patterns shift once a firm is treated as an AI developer rather than an ordinary data handler.
